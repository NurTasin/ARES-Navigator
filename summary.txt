ARES-NAVIGATOR, engineered by Team Starora, is an autonomous, physics-driven tactical mission control and surface navigation platform for human (Marswalk EVA) and robotic exploration on Mars. It directly addresses the Artemis-to-Mars challenge by bridging the gap between macro-scale planetary mapping and the hyperlocalized, meter-scale physical realities confronting surface crews.

Operating across four high-detail expedition sites (Jezero Delta, Mount Sharp, Arcadia Planitia, and Melas Chasma), ARES-NAVIGATOR fuses seven live NASA remote-sensing layers—including 1.0m HiRISE stereo DTMs, CTX panchromatic imagery, MOLA geoid elevations, and THEMIS thermal inertia—into an interconnected bicubic terrain field.

Placing astronaut safety first, the platform introduces strict bi-directional (A→B and B→A) A* route planning that enforces 0.38g rollover slope limits (>15°) and regolith wheel-slip avoidance. Its six autonomous survival engines model modified Santee-Pandolf metabolics (150W→490W) to prevent life-support exhaustion under the NASA EVA Rule of Half, ray-cast 3D cliff shelters during CME solar particle storms (RAD-Haven), track diurnal sunset freeze countdowns, and map orbital communications blackouts. Featuring an immersive 1.8m helmet-visor HUD, a context-aware AI copilot ("Nini"), and certified NASA PDS4 XML export, ARES-NAVIGATOR ensures every planetary traverse returns safely.
